Dev Note: Redesign the Clockfield landing page so it feels premium, professional, unique, and not AI-generated

Objective

Rework the new Clockfield landing page so it looks like a real premium SaaS homepage, not like a generic AI-made template.

The design direction should take structural inspiration from CompanyCam’s public site because it uses a polished SaaS layout with:
a strong top navigation,
clear Log In and Sign Up actions,
a bold hero section,
high visual confidence,
feature-based storytelling,
and repeated call-to-action placement throughout the page.

This redesign must keep Clockfield’s own product, features, branding, and workflows.
Do not copy CompanyCam.
Do not clone their branding, colors, wording, imagery, or exact layout.
Use them only as inspiration for professionalism, confidence, spacing, and flow.

Critical instruction

Do not break or affect anything else that is currently working in the app.

Do not touch:
existing login logic,
signup logic,
employee access logic,
dashboard logic,
public report logic,
task flows,
reviews,
attendance,
payroll,
admin features,
or any other current working page unless it is directly required for safe landing page routing.

If anything outside the landing page must be changed, ask for approval first.

Main problem to fix

The current landing page looks too much like an AI-generated slope.
It does not feel premium enough.
It does not feel like a serious SaaS product homepage.
It likely lacks strong layout discipline, visual hierarchy, unique composition, and brand confidence.

The new landing page must feel:
premium,
intentional,
clean,
modern,
conversion-focused,
credible,
and designed by a real product team.

It must not feel:
template-like,
AI-sloppy,
over-rounded everywhere,
randomly spaced,
too empty,
too inflated,
too toy-like,
or like a fake startup mockup.

Design direction

Overall design tone

The new landing page should feel closer to:
high-end B2B SaaS,
field operations software,
commercial operations platform,
professional product marketing site.

The vibe should be:
confident,
clean,
bold,
usable,
real.

It should have:
strong section rhythm,
proper spacing,
clean typography,
real screenshot presentation,
controlled use of gradients,
subtle premium motion,

and crisp desktop structure.

This is what CompanyCam does well from a structural perspective:
they separate public marketing from product access,
keep the nav simple,
keep primary actions visible,
use a large visual hero,
and organize feature exploration in a way that feels professional and commercial.

What to avoid

Do not build:

oversized pill shapes on every card,
generic fake mockup sections,
too many glows,

or placeholder-looking layout blocks.

Do not create a page that looks like:
a Framer template,
a fake startup landing page,
a crypto UI,
or a random AI prompt result.

The page needs to feel custom.

Landing page design system requirements

Layout width

Use a more premium desktop container width.
Recommended max width:
1280px to 1440px

Do not let sections feel too narrow.
Do not leave huge dead space on desktop.
Do not create awkward giant gaps.

Spacing system

Use a consistent spacing scale.
Examples:
section top/bottom padding should feel deliberate,
headline margins should be tight and controlled,
cards should align cleanly,
and horizontal spacing should feel premium, not random.

Typography

Use stronger professional typography hierarchy:
Hero headline should be bold and clean
Subheadline should be readable and slightly softer
Section headings should be sharp and consistent
Feature card text should be compact and scannable

Avoid text that feels too centered everywhere unless intentional.
Use left alignment where it improves professionalism.

Buttons

Buttons should feel premium and product-grade.
Avoid generic oversized round AI-template buttons.

Primary CTA:
solid, confident, brand-colored

Secondary CTA:
outlined or subtle neutral button

Button sizing:
consistent height,
clean padding,
strong hover states,
not cartoonish

Border radius

Reduce excessive radius.
The current page likely feels too soft or too “AI UI” because everything may be over-rounded.

Use:
cards with moderate radius,
buttons with controlled radius,
panels with premium corners,
not giant bubble shapes everywhere.

Shadows

Use subtle shadows only where needed.
No heavy floating-card overload.

Background strategy

The page background should not feel flat or fake.
Use layered neutral surfaces.

Recommended:
off-white or very light neutral base,
section alternation with subtle tone shifts,
light grid or texture only if elegant,
very restrained accent gradients

No noisy abstract AI background.
