Yes. I would make this a **dedicated Incident Reports module inside Clockfield**, using the existing employee authentication and Clockfield design system. It should feel like part of Clockfield, not a separate form product.

The research supports a two-part structure: first, the employee records the facts and evidence; then management reviews the report, investigates causes, adds corrective actions, signs it, and produces the final report. CCOHS recommends collecting physical evidence, photos, witness information, sequence of events and contributing factors rather than simply assigning blame. Manitoba guidance similarly expects incident reports to document who was involved, when and where it happened, what happened, evidence, causes and corrective actions. ([CCOHS][1])

## Clockfield — Incident Reports

I would add **Incident Reports** to the Clockfield admin navigation.

The workflow would be:

**Admin creates incident → Clockfield generates secure link → cleaner authenticates → cleaner completes report → cleaner signs → admin receives report → admin reviews/investigates → admin signs → final report is generated → admin sends report to client.**

### 1. Admin creates the incident request

Admin clicks:

**Incident Reports → + New Incident Report**

The creation screen should look like the rest of Clockfield and use the existing Clockfield logo, typography, buttons, cards and colours.

Admin selects:

* Client
* Location/site
* Assigned employee/cleaner
* Incident category
* Date of incident, if already known
* Approximate time, if known
* Area of building, if known
* Priority: Low / Medium / High / Critical
* Sections the cleaner must complete
* Whether photos are required
* Minimum number of photos, if appropriate
* Additional custom questions
* Internal admin note
* Delivery method: Copy Link / Email / SMS, depending on what Clockfield supports

Clockfield automatically displays:

> **Report must be submitted within 24 hours.**

The expiration should be calculated server-side from the moment the link is created.

Example:

**Created:** September 14, 2026 · 9:15 PM
**Expires:** September 15, 2026 · 9:15 PM

Admin clicks:

**Generate & Send Report**

# 5. What Happened?

This should be the most important section.

### Describe what happened

> Please describe the incident in your own words. Include what you were doing immediately before the incident, what happened, and what happened immediately afterward.

Large text field.

Then:

**What were you doing immediately before the incident?**

**What did you see or hear?**

**What happened immediately afterward?**

**What do you believe may have contributed to the incident?**

Those questions closely follow incident-investigation practices recommended by CCOHS, which emphasize establishing the sequence of events and gathering observations before reaching conclusions. ([CCOHS][1])

# 19. Reporting / Compliance Section

I would add an internal admin-only question:

### Does this incident require external reporting?

* WCB
* Workplace Safety and Health
* Insurance
* Police
* Client
* Other
* None

And:

**External Report Reference #**

This is important because **Clockfield's incident report should not be presented as replacing legally required notifications**.

For example, Manitoba requires employers to notify Workplace Safety and Health **immediately and by the fastest means available** for defined serious incidents. The internal Clockfield 24-hour link cannot be used as a reason to delay that notification. ([Province of Manitoba][4])

Clockfield could actually show administrators a warning for certain answers:

> ⚠️ **Potential Serious Incident**
> This incident may require immediate regulatory reporting. Clockfield's internal report does not replace applicable reporting requirements.

One particularly important Manitoba-specific point: serious incidents may require immediate notification to Workplace Safety and Health, and workplace injuries that require medical attention or time away can also trigger WCB reporting obligations.
