Yes. The missing piece is not “add employee.”
The missing piece is “employee access onboarding.”

Right now your app has employee records, but not employee user accounts. That is why it feels broken.

Best way to fix it without changing too much

Do not add “employee” to the public sign-up page as a totally open account type.
That can get messy fast because random people could create employee accounts without being attached to a company.

Instead, keep it like this:

Company/Admin
Creates the company account publicly

Employee
Gets invited or activated by the company

That is how a lot of workforce apps work. Current products commonly let managers add workers first, then invite them by SMS, link, QR code, phone, or email, instead of making employee sign-up fully open. Connecteam, for example, supports adding users and sending invite links by SMS or QR, and says admins can add users with a full name and cell phone number.

What your app should do now

You need to split these two things clearly:

Employee profile
This is the worker record inside the company

Employee login account
This is the credential the worker uses to sign in

Those are not the same thing.

Recommended simple structure

Option A, best for your case right now
Admin creates employee profile
Admin assigns a phone number or username
System generates an invite code or temporary PIN
Employee uses that to activate account
Employee sets their own password
Then they can log in and clock in/out

This is the cleanest fix because you said many employees may not have email. That matches how some workforce tools support alternate onboarding methods like SMS invite links, QR code access, or even phone-based access for workers without typical desktop-style accounts.
